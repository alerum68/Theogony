# Ancient DNA migration map

![Ancient DNA migration map](images/user-guide/migration-map.png)

The Ancient DNA Migration Map visualizes deep prehistoric human migration pathways, connecting your family's direct paternal (Y-DNA) and maternal (mtDNA) lineages to ancient archaeological discoveries across the globe. Open this screen when you want to explore the deep phylogeography of your haplogroups or inspect ancient DNA samples recovered from archaeological excavation sites.

## What you see

- **Lineage Mode Toggle:** Select between **Y-DNA (Paternal)** and **mtDNA (Maternal)** views.
- **Kit Selector:** Dropdown to choose which imported DNA kit to display.
- **Global Migration Map:** An interactive cartographic display showing:
  - **Migration Pathways:** Flow arrows illustrating prehistoric population movements from ancestral origin points through geographic bottlenecks into continents.
  - **Haplogroup Branch Milestones:** Labeled mutation waypoints tracking lineage diversification across millennia.
  - **Ancient DNA Archaeological Sites:** Map markers representing ancient human burials where modern genome sequencing has recovered ancient DNA.
- **Ancient Sample Inspector:** Clicking any archaeological pin opens a details card showing the site name, archaeological culture (such as *Bell Beaker*, *Corded Ware*, *Yamnaya*, or *Anglo-Saxon*), calibrated radiocarbon date (BP / BCE), and the sequenced subclade.

## Common tasks

### Inspect your paternal Y-DNA migration route

1. Set the lineage toggle to **Y-DNA (Paternal)**.
2. Select your target reference kit in the **Kit Selector**.
3. The map traces the path of your direct paternal haplogroup, from its root origins through successive defining SNP mutations, culminating in your lineage's terminal subclade.

### Explore ancient archaeological samples

1. Pan and zoom into a geographic region of interest (for example, Britain, Scandinavia, or the Eurasian Steppe).
2. Click any archaeological site marker.
3. The **Sample Inspector** displays the specimen identifier (e.g., `I0124`), burial context, radiocarbon date, and the specific haplogroup determined by researchers.

### Switch to maternal mtDNA pathways

1. Select **mtDNA (Maternal)** in the lineage toggle.
2. The map updates immediately to display maternal mitochondrial haplogroup migration routes (M, N, R, and descendant branches) and ancient female burials.

## Practical use cases

- **Deep ancestry context for family narratives:** Supplement traditional documentary family histories with deep genetic origins, explaining when and where ancestral lineages originated before written records existed.
- **Correlating archaeological cultures with surnames:** When investigating ancient origins for a Y-DNA surname project, examine ancient burial samples matching your haplogroup branch (e.g., R1b-P312 or I-M223) to understand its Bronze Age or Iron Age distribution across Europe.
- **Understanding maternal deep roots:** Trace maternal haplogroups (such as H, U, or J) across Ice Age refugia and Neolithic farming migrations into historical populations.

## Good to know

- Migration routes and ancient sample coordinates are drawn from peer-reviewed archaeogenetic studies and public ancient DNA databases.
- The Ancient DNA Migration Map operates completely offline using embedded geospatial datasets; no internet connection or remote API call is required.
- Selecting an ancient sample allows you to copy its specimen identifier and publication citation for deeper academic follow-up.
