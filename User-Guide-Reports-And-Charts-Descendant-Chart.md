# Descendant chart

The Descendant Chart displays all direct descendants of an ancestral couple, branching downward or outward across successive generations. Open this chart when you want to track a family lineage from an immigrant progenitor, document all cousins descending from a founding couple, or visualize how branches multiply over time.

## What you see

- **Progenitor Couple:** Pinned at the top (in Top-Down mode) or the left (in Left-Right mode).
- **Couples as Paired Units:** Husbands and wives are displayed together as paired side-by-side boxes on single generation rows. Multiple marriages are displayed in sequence with clear marital links.
- **Centred Children and Generational Rows:** Children are neatly centered under their parents' marriage unit on the next generational row, connected by elbow vector lines that drop down from the parental couple and branch outward to each child.
- **Subtree Spacing:** Intelligent layout algorithms ensure that large family subtrees never overlap or collide, automatically expanding spacing for prolific branches while keeping smaller branches tidy.
- **Chart Options Toolbar:**
  - **Layout Direction**: Toggle between **Top-Down (Vertical)** and **Left-Right (Horizontal)**.
  - **Generations**: Control the depth of descendant generations displayed.
  - **Spouse Display**: Option to include spouses of descendants or display direct bloodline descendants only.
  - **Color By**: Color by *Generation*, *Sex*, or *Lineage Branch*.
  - **Export PDF...**: Export to vector PDF with single-page scaling or multi-page tiled grids.

## Common tasks

### Switch between Top-Down and Left-Right layouts

1. In the chart style toolbar, select **Descendant (Top-down)** for a traditional vertical tree where generations hang downward like branches of a tree.
2. Select **Descendant (Left-right)** for wide family trees with many children, where horizontal scrolling across columns is more compact and easier to read on widescreen monitors.

### Filter generation depth

1. If a 17th-century progenitor has 3,000 descendants across 10 generations, displaying everyone at once can be overwhelming.
2. In the **Generations** selector, reduce the depth to `3` or `4` generations to focus on the immediate children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren.

### Trace a specific descendant line

1. Double-click any descendant in the chart to re-root the descendant tree on that individual, instantly focusing on their branch alone.

## Practical use cases

- **Family reunion descendant posters:** Generate a complete top-down descendant chart starting with the common great-grandparents to show every attending cousin and branch at a reunion.
- **Tracking Y-chromosome and surname continuity:** Combine descendant charts with direct male-line filtering to trace the survival and distribution of a family surname across centuries.
- **Documenting probate heirs:** When settling an estate for an ancestor who died intestate, use a descendant chart to systematically document all living descendants across child, grandchild, and great-grandchild tiers to establish legal heirs under probate law.

## Good to know

- Spouses are clearly marked with marriage dates and places when recorded in the database.
- Children appear in birth order from left to right within their respective family group.
- Descendant charts handle complex family structures—including remarriages, step-children, and multiple spouses—without broken or crossing lines.
