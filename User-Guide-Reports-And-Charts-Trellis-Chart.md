# Trellis chart

The Trellis Chart displays a comprehensive genealogical network that includes both direct ancestors/descendants and collateral relatives (aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, and cousins) across generational tiers. Open this chart when you want to explore extended family connections, map multiple marriages between intermarried families, or visualize an entire community network of up to 5,000 relatives simultaneously.

## What you see

- **Generational Tiers:** Relatives are organized into clean horizontal rows corresponding to their generation level relative to the focal individual.
- **Collateral Branches:** Unlike standard pedigree or hourglass charts that exclude non-direct lines, the Trellis chart renders collateral branches (siblings, first cousins, second cousins, and their spouses) in full context.
- **Interconnected Family Linkages:** Multiple marriages, half-sibling connections, and cousin intermarriages are drawn with explicit vector connectors linking individuals to their respective family units.
- **Chart Options Toolbar:**
  - **Generation Span**: Adjust the generational reach upwards into ancestors and downwards into descendants.
  - **Collateral Depth**: Control how broadly distant cousin branches are included.
  - **Color By**: Color by *Generation*, *Sex*, or *Lineage*.
  - **Export PDF...**: Export the entire extended network to a multi-page vector PDF.

## Common tasks

### Navigate an extended family network

1. Use your mouse scroll wheel (with **Ctrl**) to zoom out and view the broader community structure, or zoom in to inspect individual dates and names.
2. Click and drag the canvas to pan across wide collateral cousin branches.
3. Press `0` at any time to fit the entire network back into the viewport.

### Trace connections between intermarried families

1. Locate an individual who married within the family (e.g., first or second cousins marrying).
2. The Trellis chart renders their links to both their parents' branch and their spouse's branch without duplicating person boxes, clearly illustrating the loop in the family structure.

### Focus on a specific branch

1. Double-click any relative in the trellis network.
2. The entire chart smoothly re-centers with that relative as the central anchor point, recalculating generational tiers around them.

## Practical use cases

- **FAN club and community network studies:** In rural or colonial research, neighboring families frequently intermarried across multiple generations (e.g., three brothers from Family A marrying three sisters from Family B). The Trellis chart is the only layout capable of displaying all these intermarriages on a single cohesive canvas.
- **Genetic genealogy network mapping:** When analyzing shared autosomal DNA matches who share multiple cousin relationships, use the Trellis chart to visualize all collateral pathways connecting the testers to common ancestral lines.
- **Identifying missing collateral lines:** Spot branches where uncles or aunts have no recorded spouses or children, identifying areas where census and probate searches should be conducted.

## Good to know

- The Trellis layout uses a high-performance network layout engine capable of rendering up to 5,000 related individuals smoothly.
- Living individuals are automatically redacted to **Private** with all dates and places hidden.
- Exporting large trellis charts to PDF is supported via multi-page grid tiling, allowing you to print large wall charts across multiple standard sheets with alignment cut guides.
